# points-that-travel

A simulation of a community engagement system built to make engagement farming unprofitable.
Every like spends points the member earned, spent points are burned, and authors are paid from a
daily pot according to how far the engaging members' points travel through the community.

The idea and the results are written up in two posts:

- [the idea](LINK-TO-THE-FIRST-POST)
- [the math and the simulation](LINK-TO-THE-SECOND-POST)

## Run it

```
python3 sim.py            # every scenario x 5 seeds, writes results.json (~90s on 20 cores)
python3 sim.py --quick    # one seed, shorter run, as a smoke test
```

Pure Python 3 standard library. No dependencies.

## What's in the model

593 accounts over 35 daily rounds (the first 10 are warm-up):

| group | accounts | behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| novel | 60 | real members, high-quality posts |
| regular | 200 | real members, average posts |
| middle | 200 | real, attentive members with little new to add |
| pod | 12 | real people who agreed to engage with each other |
| ring | 40 | farm accounts liking each other, with high points from the old system |
| sybil + beneficiary | 80 + 1 | brand new fake accounts boosting one account |

Each scenario switches one safeguard off or changes the attacker:

- `s_*` — structural safeguards only (flags off), naive farm with many tabs open at once
- `c_*` — structural safeguards only, careful farm with real reading time, one tab at a time
- no prefix — flags on

"made-by-farm" in the output is the share of all minted points generated by farm engagement,
wherever it landed. `run.log` holds the table from the last full run.

## Limits

Attackers play fixed strategies and never adapt. Honest readers like a post with probability equal
to its quality. Moderators are a weighted coin. No new members join. The results show which
mechanisms matter and roughly how much. They don't predict numbers on a real platform.
